Revision 02

Identity refinement · Revision 02

The mark was right. The drawing wasn't finished.

Same three pads, same deep green, same stacked wordmark. What changed is everything underneath: the pad is now four tangent circles instead of a freehand curve, the two words are set to one measure, and every gap in the system is a number rather than a judgement call.

01 — Diagnosis

Seven things measured, not felt

Every number below came off the supplied artwork at its native 1959 × 803, with cap height taken from the H at 178 px.

Both lines end in an E — and the two E's don't line up.

WasThe right edge is out by 11 px on a 178 px cap: 6.2 % of cap height. The block is neither centred, flush-left nor flush-right; it is all three by accident.

NowBoth lines set to one identical measure of 531.5. Flush left, flush right, exact.

The round capitals overshoot roughly twice as far as they should.

WasO stands 5.6 % taller than H. A reference grotesk on the same cap height runs 2.9 %. HOUSE reads a size larger than VERDE.

NowOvershoot cut to 1.3 % per side, applied only to O, U and S.

Letter widths don't agree with one another.

WasAgainst a reference grotesk: V +23 %, H +16 %, O +16 %, D +15 % — but E only +3 % and S +4 %. The round and diagonal letters are inflated; the flat ones are not. That mismatch is what reads as generic.

NowWidths redrawn on one system — O narrower than tall at 0.91, V 0.90, H 0.86, D 0.88, R 0.82, S 0.74, E 0.72.

The two lines are set as two lines, not as one object.

WasLeading runs 1.35 × cap height, and tracking 0.371 × cap. Generous, but the block never resolves into a single shape.

NowLeading 1.16 × cap, tracking 0.319 × cap — 15 % tighter, still open.

The symbol's two junction gaps are 48 % apart.

WasUpper pad to main pad: 13 px. Left pad to main pad: 19.2 px. They are meant to read as the same detail. At small sizes one closes well before the other.

NowBoth junctions solved to an identical 1.56 % of symbol height.

The lower pad carries 55 % of the mark and is widest at 43 % of its height.

WasWidest above the middle, tapering to a soft point below — the silhouette of a pear, not a nopal pad.

Now50.0 %, widest at 46 %. The two smaller pads pick up what it gave away.

The symbol isn't centred on the wordmark at all.

WasIts centre sits 90.5 px — half a cap height — above the wordmark's. And its centre of ink falls 5.5 % right of its own bounding box, so it lands off-centre in any square container.

NowOptically centred on the block; centre of ink now 2.7 % off, and every square lockup is positioned from that point rather than the box.

02 — The pad

One shape, drawn from four tangent circles

A top cap circle of R 29, a base cap of R 17.5, and two flank arcs of R 68 tangent to both. Nothing in the outline is freehand, and nothing in it can drift. The pad stands 0.66 as wide as it is long and is widest 46 % of the way down.

That one pad is placed three times at a linear ratio of 1/√φ = 0.7862, so each pad's area is exactly φ times the next — 50.0 / 30.9 / 19.1 %. The old mark ran 55.2 / 25.4 / 19.4, which is why the lower pad felt heavy. Each pad is symmetric about its own axis; all of the organic character comes from the composition — three scales, three rotations (−6°, +34°, −27°), one asymmetric triangle.

03 — The capitals

Eight letters, drawn rather than licensed

V E R D H O U S. There is no font file behind the wordmark, so there is nothing to license, embed, or silently update. The forms are a modern grotesk on architectural proportions: narrow round shapes, low stroke contrast, flat-cut terminals.

The S is the one letter that isn't built from primitives. It is a variable-width offset of a single centreline: the stroke thins where it runs horizontal and thickens where it runs vertical, following the same optical rule that governs the O and the U, then gets flat horizontal terminals top and bottom. The V carries a 1.7-unit flat at its apex so the point survives embroidery and deboss.

04 — The block

Two words, one measure

VERDE and HOUSE are set to an identical measure of 531.5, so the block is a true rectangle — flush left, flush right, both lines ending on an E that aligns exactly. The letters differ in width, so the tracking differs slightly per line (VERDE 32.32, HOUSE 31.50). That is the mechanism, not an error.

Leading

1.16 × cap, down from 1.35. Enough to close the two lines into one object without letting the H crowd the V.

Tracking

0.319 × cap, 15 % tighter. Per-pair optical kerning runs 29.96 / 32.96 / 32.21 / 34.16 on VERDE — the V gives away the most white, so it takes the least space.

Overhang

The V's apex hangs 1.8 units left of the H stem, so the two lines align to the eye rather than to the ruler.

05 — Lockups

Two configurations, both measured

The symbol is centred on the block at the midpoint between its bounding centre and its centre of ink. Pure box centring reads top-heavy on a shape this bottom-weighted; pure mass centring reads sunk. Clear space on every lockup is one cap height on all four sides.

06 — Monogram

VH, with one stroke removed

For the places the cactus doesn't belong — a favicon at 16 px, a document footer, a stamped fastener. The V's right arm and the H's left stem are a single shared stroke, so the monogram is four strokes where V and H set separately would be five. Same cap height, same stem weight, same optical rules as the wordmark.

07 — Colour

Four colours. No tints.

08 — Reproduction

What happens at 16 pixels

Three pads separated by 1.56 % of the mark's height cannot stay separate below about 32 px, in a 12-stitch embroidery run, or under a deboss die. Pretending otherwise produces a blob. So the symbol ships in two cuts, and below 20 px the monogram takes over.

09 — Files

Everything rebuilds from source

Every mark is generated by tools/build_identity.py from the geometry described here. Change the leading by one unit and the whole system re-cuts consistently — there is no artwork that only exists as a file someone once drew.
